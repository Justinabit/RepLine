THE MECHANICS

How It Works

What is the challenge?

Do as many push-ups as you can, back to back, without stopping. Your score is the number of valid, consecutive repetitions counted by the camera before you rest or finish.

How does the camera count reps?

REP LINE runs on-device pose detection (MediaPipe Pose Landmarker) to find your shoulders, elbows, wrists and hips 30+ times a second. It measures your elbow angle over time and only counts a rep after a full up → down → up cycle — never from a single frame or a small twitch.

What counts as a valid push-up?

Starting near-straight arms (top position), lowering until your elbow angle passes the bottom threshold, then pushing back up past the top threshold again. Partial dips, wiggling, or standing up don't complete the cycle, so they aren't counted.

How should I position my camera?

Prop your phone or laptop at roughly waist height, to your side, far enough back that your whole body — shoulders to ankles — fits in frame. Landscape orientation usually works best on phones.

Why wasn't my rep counted?

Usually one of: part of your body left the frame, lighting was too low for confident tracking, or the movement didn't reach a full top or bottom position. Watch the on-screen tracking quality and form feedback — they'll tell you what to fix in real time.

How is the leaderboard calculated?

Highest completed-challenge score wins, sorted descending, with ties sharing a rank. It's a local leaderboard stored in this browser — see the leaderboard page for what that means and what it would take to make it fully shared.

PRIVACY POLICY

Privacy Policy

How REP LINE uses your camera, what data is stored, and what stays only on your device.

Overview

REP LINE is a camera-based push-up tracker. This policy explains how your camera is used, what information is stored, and how the leaderboard works.

Camera Usage

REP LINE only accesses your camera after you choose to start a challenge and your browser grants permission. The camera feed is used exclusively to detect your body position and count push-up repetitions — nothing else.

How Pose Detection Works

REP LINE uses MediaPipe's pose detection technology to identify body landmarks — like your shoulders, elbows, wrists, and hips — from the camera feed. These landmarks are used to calculate movement and recognize completed push-ups, live, frame by frame, while you're tracking.

Camera Data

Camera-based pose detection is performed locally in your browser. The camera feed is used to calculate body movement and push-up repetitions, and is not intended to be recorded or saved as video, uploaded, or shared with other users.

Challenge Data

Separately from camera footage, REP LINE does store information related to your challenges: your push-up count, challenge duration and date, personal best, and challenge history. This is just numbers and text — never video or images.

Supabase & Data Storage

If the shared leaderboard is enabled, REP LINE uses Supabase — a third-party database service — to store the challenge data described above, such as your display name and best score, so it can be shown on the leaderboard. Camera video is not uploaded to Supabase for the purpose of recording or storing your push-up sessions. Otherwise, data like your history and personal records is stored locally in your browser using localStorage and stays on your device.

Leaderboard Visibility

If you take part in the shared leaderboard, your display name and push-up score may be visible to other players. Your camera feed and camera video are never displayed on the leaderboard — only your name and score.

Browser Permissions

Your browser will ask for permission before REP LINE can access your camera. You can deny permission at any time. If you deny it, the push-up camera tracker won't be available, but you can still browse the dashboard, leaderboard, history, and other parts of the site.

User Controls

You can stop camera access at any time by pausing or finishing your challenge, or by navigating away from the tracking screen — REP LINE releases the camera as soon as it's no longer needed. You can also revoke camera permission at any time through your browser's site settings.

Third-Party Services

REP LINE relies on a few third-party services: MediaPipe for on-device pose detection, Supabase for storing challenge and leaderboard data, and Vercel for hosting and deploying the website. None of these services receive your camera footage — MediaPipe's pose detection runs locally in your browser, and only challenge data, never video, is ever sent to Supabase.

Data Security

We take reasonable steps to protect the information used by REP LINE. However, no internet service can guarantee absolute security.

Changes to This Privacy Policy

This policy may be updated as REP LINE changes. Continued use of the website after changes means you accept the updated policy. Significant changes to how the camera is used will be reflected here.

How REP LINE uses your camera, what it stores, and what stays off your device entirely.

1. Overview

REP LINE is a camera-based push-up tracker. This policy explains, plainly, how your camera is used and what information the app stores so you can decide whether you're comfortable using it.

2. Camera Usage

Your camera is used only to detect and count push-up movements while you're actively using the tracker. REP LINE does not intentionally record, photograph, save, upload, or share your camera footage, and does not use your camera for anything unrelated to push-up tracking.

3. How Pose Detection Works

REP LINE uses MediaPipe's pose detection technology to identify body landmarks (like your shoulders, elbows, wrists, and hips) from the live camera feed. Those landmarks are used to figure out your body position and recognize when you've completed a valid push-up. This detection runs locally in your browser — your device does the calculation itself.

4. Camera Data

REP LINE does not need to record your camera video to count push-ups, and does not do so. The video frames are analyzed for pose landmarks in the moment and are not saved as a video file, image, or recording — on your device or anywhere else.

5. Challenge Data

What is saved is your challenge results — things like your rep count, challenge duration, date, and whether it was a personal record. This is ordinary app data, not camera footage, and is stored in your browser's local storage on your device.

6. Supabase & Data Storage

If the site's shared leaderboard is enabled, REP LINE uses Supabase to store the data needed for that feature — specifically your display name and best score. Camera video is never uploaded to Supabase, or anywhere else, for the purpose of recording or storing your push-up sessions.

7. Leaderboard Visibility

If you take part in a shared leaderboard, your display name and push-up score may be visible to other players. Your camera feed and camera video are never displayed on the leaderboard, or anywhere else — only the numbers are shared.

8. Browser Permissions

Your browser will ask for permission before REP LINE can access your camera. You can deny permission at any time. If you deny it, or haven't granted it yet, the push-up camera tracker won't be available, but the rest of the site — your dashboard, history, and leaderboard — still works normally.

9. User Controls

You can stop camera access at any point by pausing or finishing your challenge, or by navigating away from the tracking screen — REP LINE releases the camera as soon as it's no longer needed. You can also revoke camera permission entirely through your browser's own site settings.

10. Third-Party Services

REP LINE relies on a small number of third-party services: MediaPipe for on-device pose detection, Supabase (optional) for shared leaderboard data, and Vercel for hosting the website itself. None of these receive your camera footage.

11. Data Security

We take reasonable steps to protect the information REP LINE stores. However, no internet service can guarantee absolute security, and we can't promise one either.

12. Changes to This Privacy Policy

If this policy changes, the update will be reflected on this page. Meaningful changes to how the camera is used would only ever move toward more transparency, not less.
